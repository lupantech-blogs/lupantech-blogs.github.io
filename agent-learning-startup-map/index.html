01 / The map

Agent relevance is broad. Evidence of learning needs a narrower lens.

We reviewed the 92 companies in a September 16, 2026 snapshot of Breakout List, then expanded beyond the list descriptions into company websites, product documentation and research posts. The question for Noeri is practical: which companies build agents, which supply their infrastructure, and where does experience actually change the system?

The review identifies 34 companies with agent products, models or explicit research programs, and 22 with agent tools or integrations. Another seven provide general AI infrastructure. That gives us 56 directly relevant products or enablers, or 63 if we include indirect infrastructure. A trading API, an RL platform and an autonomous research agent occupy different positions in this map; they are not interchangeable competitors.

For learning, the positive set is smaller: 14 companies describe feedback-driven updates, three articulate a self-improvement research direction, and 17 provide training or evaluation tools. Together these are 34 learning-relevant companies. This is not a count of 34 autonomous, self-improving agents.

02 / What changes after feedback?

“Self-improvement” describes several different update targets.

A useful first question is not whether a company uses the term agent. It is what persists after an attempt ends. Does feedback change weights, a policy, code, a playbook, a tool, or the next experimental decision? Then ask how that change is evaluated and who approves it.

Models & policies

New experience becomes training data.

Trajectory describes optimizing from product usage with evaluation and approval before deployment. Engram describes recurring training on changing workspace context. Physical Intelligence publishes online RL and robot-policy learning from experience.

XDOF’s SPIRAL is a particularly concrete research example: robots collect attempts, a reward model scores them, and the policy is refined over successive rounds after an initial human-labeling step. This supports a bounded learning-loop claim, not a claim of general recursive self-improvement.

Question for Noeri: Can the update improve held-out behavior rather than merely fit the reward or the latest batch of experience?

Agent code & knowledge

The system around the model changes.

Factory Signals connects session friction to issues and Droid-generated fixes. Lovable describes agent feedback producing knowledge entries and code-change proposals. Its account explicitly retains human review and says the loop is not fully closed.

Cognition documents session analysis that creates or revises playbooks. Serval uses ticket data to build automation tools and suggest knowledge repairs, with admins reviewing and publishing workflows.

Question for Noeri: Which editable artifact captures a reusable improvement, and what regression test must it pass before promotion?

Scientific experiments

Results determine the next attempt.

Medra describes feedback-driven protocol optimization in robotic experiments. Quadrillion’s Qualia plans experiments, interprets their results and chooses new research directions, including model-improvement tasks. Updating an experimental plan is meaningful, but does not establish that the agent retrains itself.

Periodic Labs provides a different kind of evidence: its September 15 research post reports training Periodic Neon on laboratory data and deploying it to analyze experiments. Broader autonomous scientific campaigns remain a development direction.

Question for Noeri: How do we separate improvement in the scientist, improvement in its tools, and a better choice of experiment?

Research agendas & enablers

Relevance can precede a deployed loop.

Ricursive explicitly targets a loop between AI chip design and the hardware that supports AI. Standard Intelligence and Core Automation describe learning-oriented research agendas. We keep research direction separate from feedback mechanisms described in products or demonstrations.

Convergent and Braintrust make production behavior usable for evaluation and optimization. Fireworks, Modal and SF Compute supply parts of the training or execution layer. Infrastructure is relevant without itself being a self-improving agent.

Question for Noeri: Which parts of a reliable improvement cycle should we build, and which can we obtain from existing tools?

03 / A working agenda for Noeri

Compare complete improvement cycles, not agent labels.

The market scan suggests a useful research agenda. These are proposals for discussion among Noeri members, not statements about Noeri’s current implementation, roadmap or competitive advantage.

1. Make the update target explicit.

A model fine-tune, a harness patch and a revised skill should not share one undifferentiated “improvement” score. Record what changed, what evidence motivated it, and the previous artifact it replaces. Start by comparing systems with similar update targets.

2. Treat the evaluator as part of the system under study.

A learning loop is only as informative as its feedback. Track who or what supplies the signal, whether the evaluator sees held-out tasks, and whether optimizing it creates regressions elsewhere. XDOF’s reward-model work and the evaluation layers at Braintrust and Convergent make this dependency concrete.

3. Measure the whole cycle.

For a controlled comparison, freeze the initial agent, model route, tools, data split and resource budget. Measure held-out task success before and after the update, as well as training or search cost, elapsed time, failed attempts and human interventions. Report stochastic variation. The startup counts in this article cannot answer those performance questions.

4. Include human approval in the analysis.

Human-reviewed loops can be operationally useful. Their approval cost and response time are also part of the design. Factory, Lovable, Trajectory and Serval are useful cases to examine because the review or deployment boundary is visible. Calling a system autonomous should specify which stages still depend on a person.

5. Start with a small, mechanism-based reading list.

For model and policy updates, begin with Trajectory, Engram, Physical Intelligence and XDOF. For code and reusable knowledge, begin with Factory, Lovable, Cognition and Serval. For scientific feedback, begin with Periodic Labs, Medra and Quadrillion. For evaluation infrastructure, examine Braintrust and Convergent. This is a reading shortlist, not a ranking or a recommendation to buy.

04 / How to read the counts

Deeper research reduced uncertainty; it did not erase it.

The initial screen relied heavily on list descriptions. It left agent relevance unestablished for 56 companies and learning relevance unestablished for 76. The expanded review found positive agent-related evidence for another 27 companies, including five indirect infrastructure providers, and positive learning relevance for another 18.

Using the same broad positive totals, agent relevance moves from 36 to 63 and learning relevance from 16 to 34. The expanded taxonomy also separates a known business focus from missing technical evidence. Relabeling a company as “other documented focus” is not counted as new positive evidence.

Learning relevance remains unestablished for 58 companies: 32 have AI-related activity without a qualifying loop in the reviewed sources, 24 have another documented focus, and two have insufficient public detail. The latter are Long Lake and OpenEvidence; sparse or inaccessible company information limited this review. These labels describe the evidence collected, not everything a company may do internally.

Classification rules and boundaries
  • Unit: one company, counted once in each dimension. Categories are assigned by the strongest qualifying public evidence in this review; they do not enumerate every product.
  • Agent products / models: software agents, embodied autonomous systems, specific agent features, and explicit agent research programs. This broad scope includes systems with very different levels of capability and autonomy.
  • Agent tools / integrations: agent development, runtime, evaluation, security, retrieval or external tool access. A narrow agent-access feature qualifies without making the company an agent-native business.
  • General AI infrastructure: indirect support such as compute, hardware or general training data. Kept separate from direct products and agent-specific tools.
  • Feedback-driven updates: a described path from experience or results to changed models, policies, code, knowledge, tools or experimental decisions. Includes human-reviewed workflows and bounded research demonstrations. It does not require autonomous weight updates.
  • Research direction: an explicit self-improvement agenda without an established deployed loop in the reviewed sources.
  • Training / evaluation tools: an enabling input or capability. It is not evidence that the provider’s own product self-improves.
  • AI, no loop documented: agent execution, inference, retrieval, saved preferences or adaptive control without a documented qualifying update path.
  • Other documented focus: sources establish another business focus. This is not a claim that the company has no internal AI.
  • Evidence standard: company product pages, documentation and research reports; Cambridge Aerospace uses an investor’s portfolio description. OpenEvidence remains list-only because company-site access was limited. Claims were not independently reproduced.
  • Sampling: a curated list snapshot, not a representative sample of all startups. Counts are not market share, investment merit, hiring quality or proof of technical leadership. Company size and location are inherited from Breakout List and were not separately audited.
  • Coverage: all 92 original company sites were attempted, followed by targeted source review. This is bounded public research, not an exhaustive audit of every product or internal system.
